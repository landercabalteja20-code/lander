# Style guide for the next episodes

Feedback after Ep. 1 (ants): **3D models must be more diverse and full of life, especially colour.**

## What fell short in Ep. 1
- People were identical "capsule pawns": same height/build, plain one-colour shirts, no faces.
- Scenes were dominated by one hue (all-orange cat, all-teal carpet, dark-grey storm).
- Motion was mostly locomotion; characters had no expressions or secondary motion.

## Characters: diversity
- Mix of ages (kids, teens, adults, elderly), heights and body shapes (slim, broad, round, tall, short).
- Wide range of skin tones and hair: curly, braids, buns, bald, ponytail, afro, hijab, caps, beards, grey hair.
- Varied outfits: patterned shirts (stripes, checks, florals), jackets, dresses, overalls, hoodies, sneakers in bright colours,
  accessories (glasses, backpacks, scarves, headphones).
- Simple but expressive faces: eyes with highlights, eyebrows, blinking, mouths (smile / shock / scream).
- Build a character generator (seeded) so every crowd is varied automatically, with no two people the same.

## Colour: life
- Saturated, complementary palettes per scene (e.g. warm key light + cool shadows, teal/orange, pink/lime, purple/gold).
- Every scene gets at least one strong accent colour against its base; avoid single-hue frames.
- Coloured lights: warm sun, cool fill and a coloured rim light so characters pop off the background.
- More colour variety in props and nature: flowers, berries, toys, candy, colourful packaging, bugs (ladybugs, beetles).
- A final colour grade pass (contrast + vibrance) on every scene.

## Life: motion and detail
- Secondary motion: hair/clothes sway, breathing, head turns, blinking, reactions to events.
- Crowds doing different things at the same time (pointing, hugging, running, sitting, waving).
- Environmental life: floating dust/pollen, moving leaves, flickering light, small creatures in the background.
- More props per shot so the world feels lived-in.
