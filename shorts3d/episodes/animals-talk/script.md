# What if animals could somehow talk?

Format: narrated 3D short, built from `references/zackdfilms-3d-shorts/STYLE.md`
(story shape A: escalating consequences told as fact). ~44 s, 1080×1920, 30 fps.

One narrator carries the story at ~2.9 words/s. The one deliberate change from the reference format: the
animals get a few very short lines, because hearing them talk *is* the premise. Setup and payoff: the cat
stays silent early, and the last line explains why.

## Lines

| # | Who | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Narrator | If animals could suddenly talk, your dog would wake you up at five a.m. to say one word. |
| 2 | Dog | Walk. Walk? WALK! |
| 3 | Narrator | You see, the average dog knows about a hundred and sixty-five words, and it would use all of them to ask for food. |
| 4 | Narrator | Your cat, strangely, wouldn't say a thing. |
| 5 | Narrator | The problem is, Earth has one and a half billion cows, and they'd all have questions about burgers. |
| 6 | Cow | We need to talk. |
| 7 | Narrator | Pigeons would roast everyone from the rooftops. |
| 8 | Pigeon | Nice bald spot! |
| 9 | Narrator | And your parrot would repeat everything you ever said about your boss… to your boss. |
| 10 | Parrot (in your voice) | Gary smells like soup! |
| 11 | Narrator | But when someone finally asked the cat why it never said a word… |
| 12 | Cat | Oh, we could always talk. We just didn't want to. |

Facts used: the average dog can learn about 165 words (Stanley Coren, 2009); the world has roughly
1.5 billion cattle (FAO).
