# What if people never normalized wearing clothes?

A 3-minute day in a world where nobody wears clothes (2:59.7, 24 fps, 1080×1920), in the narrated-3D format from
[`references/zackdfilms-3d-shorts/STYLE.md`](../../../references/zackdfilms-3d-shorts/STYLE.md): one narrator
(~2.9 words/s), a new picture every 1–2 s, numbers built as 3D objects, a thought bubble, a twist in the last line.

## Keeping it family-friendly

- The people are smooth, doll-like cartoon bodies with no anatomy at all (`tools/people.py`).
- The world supplies its own coverage: everyone wears a fanny pack (no pockets), fashion is body paint (painted
  dresses, a painted-on tuxedo), and the clothes that survive are tools: aprons, coats, uniform hats, ties.
- Every shot of an unclothed character is framed waist-up or has a prop in front (desk, counter, copier, seat
  backs, pack, apron, blanket).
- The Greek race uses a TV-style pixel mosaic (`shot.censor` in `engine/engine.js`) as the visual gag.
- No children appear.

## Facts used

- Clothing as early as ~170,000 years ago, dated from when body lice split from head lice (Toups et al., 2011).
- *Gymnasium* comes from Greek *gymnos*, "naked"; ancient Olympic athletes competed nude; the legend of
  Orsippus of Megara losing his loincloth in 720 BC.
- Naturist etiquette: always sit on your own towel.
- About one ounce (a shot glass) of sunscreen covers the body; reapply every two hours (AAD guidance).
- Thermoneutral temperature for an unclothed resting adult is roughly 27–32 °C (≈82 °F).
- Global apparel market ≈ $1.7 trillion a year; ~2,700 L of water per cotton T-shirt, about 2.5 years of
  drinking water (WWF); ~300 laundry loads per family per year (ENERGY STAR).
