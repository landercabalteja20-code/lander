A reference for the next "In this town…" Roblox-style story video. It is built from four TikToks by **@zaylento.yt** (saved November 2026). The guide covers the format, script, voice, look, editing, captions and sound, with numbers measured from the actual videos.

## 1. The formula

All four videos use the same skeleton:

```
0:00 ─ HOOK ──────── "In this town, [one impossible rule]."  (+ stakes line)
0:04 ─ RULE IN ACTION  3 escalating examples with numbers, then 1–2 "proof" characters
0:25 ─ "BUT ME?" ──── first-person kid/teen hero: "My name is ___, and ___."
0:30 ─ ESCALATION ─── 3 attempts / a chain of things going wrong, each with a punchline
1:35 ─ LOW POINT ──── one quiet, emotional line
1:45 ─ TWIST ──────── a message / note / reveal flips everything
2:00 ─ ENDING ─────── moral flip · cliffhanger · viewer vote · promo  → cut on the last word
```

| Beat | When | What happens | Example from the references |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00–0:04 | "In this town," + a rule you understand in one breath, said over an aerial shot of the town | "In this town, you get paid for being unlucky." |
| Stakes (optional) | 0:04–0:08 | Why the rule matters | "And if the lights go out for even one second, it's over for all of us." |
| Rule in action | 0:04–0:25 | Three fast examples that escalate, with hard numbers, then "proof" characters | "Miss your bus? $100. Get caught in the rain? $500. And if your house gets destroyed? $1,000,000!" |
| "But me?" | 0:14–0:37 | The hero is the exception, or has a personal stake | "But me? My name is Milo, and I have a huge problem. I've never been unlucky." |
| Escalation | 0:30–1:30 | Three tries, or a chain of failures, each ending on a punchline. A contrast character gets what the hero wants without trying. An official place confirms the problem | "$0 again." / "Sir, you have 0% bad luck." |
| Low point | ~1:35 | Quiet line, sad close-up | "I sat on my bed and whispered, why can't one bad thing happen to me?" |
| Twist | ~1:40–2:00 | A phone message, a note under a door, or the monster has moved | "Lucas, don't take gold. Trust me, Ruby." |
| Ending | last 5–15 s | One of the four ending types below | "Because if those lights go out again, he's dead." |

## 2. Writing rules (how the script sounds)

- **Open with "In this town,"** every time. The rule must fit in about 4 seconds.
- **First person, kid or teen hero** ("because I was still a kid", "tomorrow, I turn 16"). Give the name early: "My name is ___, and ___."
- **Short sentences.** The story videos average 7 words per sentence (median 6–7, longest about 20). One idea per sentence, one shot per sentence.
- **Numbers everywhere, and they escalate.** Prices ($100 → $500 → $1,000,000), counts ("six massive floodlights", "12 guards", "struck by lightning three times"), times ("2:13 AM"), percentages ("0% bad luck").
- **Rule of three.** Three examples, three attempts, three proof characters.
- **Deadpan punchlines that repeat a pattern.** "$0." then "$0 again." then "I was still broke."
- **Direct dialogue from side characters**, with no "he said" when it's obvious. "Relax, we've still got five." / "Sir, they're overheating."
- **Sound words as their own sentence.** "Boom!" / "Then, click." / "Finally!"
- **Turn words keep it moving.** But… / Then… / Suddenly… / Until… / Meanwhile… / And just like that…
- **Funny-dark tone.** Bad things happen to side characters in a cartoon way ("His private jet crashed into his swimming pool. He earned $50,000,000!").
- **Call back to the hook at the end.** "I was finally the richest person in town." / "…the next Roblox story everyone in this town is watching will be yours."
- **Tense.** Past tense for a told story (01–03). Present tense when you want it to feel live, like 04 before a viewer vote ("The next morning, a black limousine arrives.").
- **Length.** 315–410 words gives 1:48–2:25.

## 3. The voice (narration)

Measured from the four voiceovers:

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall pace | 164–176 words per minute, pauses included |
| Pace while talking | about 210–217 words per minute, which is fast but clear |
| Gap between sentences | 0.40–0.47 s median, rarely over 1 s |
| Start | the first word is at 0:00, with no intro or silence |
| End | the video cuts 0.3–0.5 s after the last word |
| Pitch | young male narrator. Median pitch is about 170–200 Hz, higher than a typical adult man. The range is wide (about 110–285 Hz), so the delivery is very expressive |
| Dynamics | very even loudness (loudness range 2.6–5 LU), so the voice is heavily compressed |

**Delivery**
- Energetic storyteller, like telling a friend something wild that happened at school.
- Hit the numbers and the twist words. Rise on questions ("But me?"). Go flat and quick on deadpan punchlines ("$0 again.").
- Drop almost to a whisper on the low point, then snap back to full energy on "Suddenly…".
- The same narrator reads the side characters' lines with a small change in tone. You don't need extra voice actors.
- No dramatic pauses longer than about 1 s. The speed is part of the style.

**How to get it**
- **Your own voice:** record close to the mic in a soft, quiet room (a closet full of clothes works). Then apply noise reduction, then strong compression, then a slight speed-up (1.05–1.15×) until you're near 210 wpm while talking. Cut the gaps between sentences down to about 0.4 s.
- **An AI voice:** pick a young, upbeat male narrator. If the tool has an expressiveness or stability setting, lean expressive. Generate the whole script in one pass so the tone stays consistent, then tighten the gaps to about 0.4 s.

## 4. Look and world

- **World:** a toy-like 3D city in the Roblox style. Blocky avatars, chunky round trees, pastel buildings, glossy plastic materials, soft sunlight, glow on lights, and a little background blur. It looks like a polished animated film, not raw gameplay. The creator says the videos are "brought to life with AI" (reference 01).
- **Characters:** cube heads with 2D cartoon faces (big oval eyes, thick eyebrows that carry the emotion, a simple mouth). Chunky 3D hair, solid-color hoodies or jackets, jeans, white sneakers.
  - The hero wears **one outfit the whole video** (02: light-blue hoodie; 04: red jacket), so you always know who the hero is.
  - Side characters are instant types by costume: guard (navy uniform, cap, radio), official (white uniform with gold trim), worker (teal uniform), mechanic (orange), grandma (white hair, glasses).
- **Every beat is sold by a facial expression:** shocked "O" mouth, smug half-smile, angry brows, sad frown.
- **Info lives inside the world, not in overlays:**
  - money pop-ups: a white-rimmed pill badge, mint fill (`#6FFFC5`), a gold coin, "+$10,000"
  - giant screens: "NEW BALANCE: $10,000,000!", "BAD LUCK: 0%"
  - building signs: "BAD LUCK CENTER", "PILL SELECTION CENTER", "LUXURY DISTRICT", "RESTRICTED"
  - countdowns: "00:10 MAKE YOUR CHOICE" in big red digits
  - phone screens and handwritten notes, shown from the hero's point of view

## 5. Camera and shots

- 9:16 vertical with the subject centered. Keep faces in the upper and middle part of the frame, because the captions sit at about 72% of the height.
- **Shot 1 is always an aerial over the town** (a drone looking down a main street or over a plaza) while you say "In this town…".
- Shot types that keep coming back:

| Shot | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Aerial / establishing | the hook, new locations, "everyone in this town…" lines |
| Street-level wide | examples of the rule in action |
| Medium shot, hero facing camera | "But me? My name is…" |
| Face close-up | every emotional beat and reaction |
| POV insert | phone screens, notes, letters, screen readouts |
| Object insert | pills, money, flashlight, lottery ticket |
| Low angle | threats and authority (monster, official) |

- The camera is always moving a little (slow push-in, drift or orbit). There are almost no fully static frames.

---

## 6. Editing and pacing

- **One shot per sentence.** The average shot lasts 2.0–2.4 s (45–68 cuts per video).

| Ref | Cuts | Avg shot |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | 45 | 2.4 s |
| 02 | 68 | 2.2 s |
| 03 | 56 | 2.4 s |
| 04 | 64 | 2.0 s |

- **Hard cuts only.** No transitions, title cards, logos or intro.
- **Show exactly what's being said.** "while walking their dogs" gets a dog-walking shot; "he accidentally wrote lava instead of math" gets a chalkboard that says LAVA.
- Cut on sentence boundaries, so the next shot starts with the next sentence.
- End right after the last word.

## 7. Captions (exact spec)

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Words on screen | **one at a time**, timed to the voice; each stays up until the next word appears (no blank gaps) |
| Case | as spoken in lowercase; capitals only for names ("Milo", "Roblox"); numbers as numerals ("$1 million", "$10,000", "0%") |
| Font | heavy geometric sans-serif, the Poppins ExtraBold/Black look (Montserrat Black is a close substitute) |
| Fill | neon yellow **`#E1FE00`** |
| Outline | thin black stroke, about 3–4 px at 1080×1920 |
| Shadow | soft black drop shadow, down and to the right, about 50% opacity |
| Size | a 7-letter word spans about 38% of the frame width (roughly 95–105 px font size at 1080×1920) |
| Position | centered horizontally, at about **72% of the frame height** (y ≈ 1390 px of 1920) |
| Animation | each word pops in: about 40% → 115% → 100% scale over about 3 frames (0.1 s), with no exit animation |

**In CapCut:** generate auto captions, set them to show one word at a time, apply the font, fill, stroke and shadow above, add a short pop or scale-in animation, and apply the style to all captions.

---

## 8. Sound

- The voice is always on top and always clear.
- A **continuous music bed** runs under the whole video, about 5–9 dB below the voice. Voice measures about −22 dBFS RMS and the gaps between sentences about −28 dBFS, so the music never drops out.
- Keep the voice dry and close-sounding, with no room echo.
- Overall loudness is very even. Aim for roughly −14 LUFS on export, a common target for short-form video, with peaks under −1 dB.
- Suggested (not measured): add sound effects on the big words, like "Boom!", thunder, coins, or "click".

## 10. Make it yours

Copy the format, not the episodes. Write your own town rules, characters and names (not Milo, Lucas or Ruby). Don't reuse the reference scripts, the creator's handle or watermark, or their course branding. TikTok also holds back reuploads and near-copies from the For You feed, so original premises are what get you reach.

## Production checklist

- [ ] The premise passes the 4-second test: "In this town, ___."
- [ ] Script is 320–400 words, sentences around 7 words, numbers in the rules
- [ ] Hero is named in the first ~25 s ("But me? My name is…")
- [ ] 3 escalating examples and 3 attempts
- [ ] Low point, then a twist in the last 30 s, then the chosen ending type
- [ ] Voiceover at about 210 wpm while talking, about 0.4 s gaps, first word at 0:00
- [ ] One shot per sentence (about 50–65 shots); shot 1 is an aerial of the town
- [ ] A face close-up on every emotional beat; the hero keeps one outfit
- [ ] Numbers shown as in-world graphics (pop-ups, screens, signs)
- [ ] Captions: one word at a time, `#E1FE00`, black stroke and shadow, 72% height, pop-in
- [ ] Music bed under the voice the whole time
- [ ] Export 1080×1920 at 30 fps; cut on the last word
